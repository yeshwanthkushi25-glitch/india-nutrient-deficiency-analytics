1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Historically, public health initiatives across developing nations focused primarily on resolving caloric famine. In modern India, subsidized agricultural supply chains have largely achieved general energy sufficiency, with average daily caloric fulfillment reaching 99.6% – 102.4% of requirements. However, empirical public health data reveals a profound paradox: over 74.6% of the population fails to achieve the ICMR-NIN recommended dietary protein allowance (0.83 g/kg/day), while 56.6% suffer from iron-deficiency anemia, 75.6% suffer from vitamin D deficiency, and 66.6% suffer from vitamin B12 deficiency.

This condition—termed The Hidden Hunger Paradox or The Double Burden of Malnutrition—manifests when individuals consume abundant calories through refined carbohydrates (70.2% of total daily energy) and fats without fulfilling cellular requirements for tissue repair, immune vitality, and neuro-cognitive development. Statistical correlation indicates a potent inverse relationship (r = -0.916) between state Nutritional Literacy Scores and Hidden Hunger Severity, demonstrating that dietary awareness is the single most decisive factor in plate balance.

2. Public Health Standards & Benchmarks

The project anchors its analytical baseline on three authoritative epidemiological benchmarks:

  • ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024): Establishes recommended allowances including 0.83 g/kg protein (54g male, 46g female), capping carbohydrate energy at 45%–55%, 1000 mg/day calcium, 19–29 mg/day iron, and 600 IU vitamin D.
  • National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-21): Comprehensive state-level empirical data on anemia across children (67.1%) and women (57.0%), stunting (35.5%), wasting (19.3%), and adult obesity.
  • Comprehensive National Nutrition Survey (CNNS, UNICEF/MoHFW): Direct biochemical serum assays for micronutrient biomarkers including ferritin, B12, 25(OH)D, and zinc.

3. Data Cleaning, Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

The raw dataset logged 6,642 records with intentional real-world artifacts in Dataset/raw_dataset.csv. The cleaning pipeline (data_cleaning.py) performed:

  • Deduplication: Identified and eliminated 162 duplicate rows (reducing dataset from 6,642 to 6,480 clean rows).
  • State Name Standardization: Standardized historic and misspelled variants (e.g., 'Orissa' → 'Odisha', 'Uttaranchal' → 'Uttarakhand', 'Pondicherry' → 'Puducherry') and mapped to official ISO-3166-2 codes.
  • Missing Value Imputation: 370 missing values were imputed using demographic stratum medians (Zone × Awareness Tier × Diet), achieving 100% complete data with 0 NaNs.
  • Feature Engineering: Computed Protein_Deficit_Pct, Caloric_Sufficiency_Pct, Hidden_Hunger_Severity_Index, and Awareness_to_Deficiency_Ratio.

4. Statistical Findings & Exploratory Data Analysis

Statistical evaluation via Data_analysis.py established the following core insights:

Key Finding 1: The Negative Correlation of Awareness
Nutritional Literacy Score correlates negatively with Hidden Hunger Severity at r = -0.916 (p < 0.001), with Anemia at r = -0.532, and with Protein Deficit at r = -0.544. Improving dietary literacy directly curbs empty-carb dependence.
Key Finding 2: Zonal Disparities
The Central (77.7/100) and Eastern (77.8/100) zones exhibit the highest Hidden Hunger Severity, driven by polished rice monoculture and lower literacy scores (33.7–38.4). The Southern Zone achieves higher literacy (52.0) and lower severity (68.3), led by Kerala (56.7).

5. High-Impact, Affordable Indian Food Swaps

The project developed a personalized plate simulation showing that overcoming micronutrient gaps does not demand costly commercial supplements. By utilizing indigenous Indian kitchen staples, families can achieve balance for pennies:

  • Sattu Drink (Roasted Bengal Gram): Swapping 1 refined roti for 1 glass of sattu adds 12g bioavailable plant protein and iron for approximately ₹10.
  • Lemon / Ascorbic Acid Acidification: Squeezing fresh lemon over dal and green sabzi triples plant (non-heme) iron absorption by converting ferric to ferrous iron.
  • Chai / Coffee Timing: Delaying tea consumption by 45 minutes after meals prevents polyphenols and tannins from chelating up to 60% of dietary iron.
  • Ragi (Finger Millet) & Sesame (Til): Ragi provides 344 mg calcium per 100g, while til provides 975 mg/100g, eliminating calcium deficits.
